Imprisonment and Crime Patterns

Analysis of violent crime and property crime imprisonment trends

Figure 3: Violent Crime Imprisonment

Assault dominates violent crime in Australia's prison system, with steady increases over the past decade.

Figure 4: Property Crime Trends

Theft shows declining imprisonment rates while robbery remains relatively stable.

Figure 5: Crime Imprisonment Comparison (2024)

Key Insights

Assault dominates: With over 12,000 prisoners, assault accounts for the largest share of imprisonment in Australia.

Violent crimes lead: Sexual assault (7,352) and homicide (3,354) represent significant portions of the prison population.

Property vs violent: Violent crimes result in substantially higher imprisonment rates than property crimes like theft and robbery.

Violent crimes, especially assault, accounted for the largest share of imprisonment in Australia in 2024, highlighting the continuing impact of violent offending on the justice system.

Demographic Analysis

Age and gender patterns in imprisonment rates

Figure 7: Imprisonment Rate by Age & Gender (2024)

Key Findings

Peak imprisonment age: Males aged 25-34 show the highest imprisonment rates, exceeding 700 per 100,000 population.

Gender disparity: Male imprisonment rates are approximately 15-20 times higher than female rates across all age groups.

Age pattern: Imprisonment rates decline sharply after age 35, with the lowest rates observed in those aged 65 and over.

Indigenous Incarceration & Social Justice

Examining systemic disparities and outcomes

Figure 8: Indigenous vs Non-Indigenous Imprisonment

Critical Social Issue

Widening gap: Indigenous imprisonment rates have surged 35% since 2015, while non-Indigenous rates fell 6%.

Systemic disparity: Indigenous Australians are imprisoned at rates 14 times higher than non-Indigenous Australians.

Urgent reform needed: This over-representation highlights deep systemic issues requiring immediate policy intervention and social justice reform.

Figure 9: Deaths in Custody (2020-2024)

Male deaths in custody significantly outnumber female deaths across all years.

Figure 10: Home Protection Methods (2024)

Deadlocks and security screens are the most common home protection measures across Australia.

Note: Deadlocks and security screens are highlighted because they are the two most commonly used home protection methods in Australia, with usage rates exceeding 50% of households.

Systemic Issue

Indigenous Australians are 14 times more likely to be imprisoned than non-Indigenous Australians, despite making up only 3.8% of the population. This over-representation has increased over the past decade.
